# Authored reference translation

Use an admired artist, studio, campaign, or poster as design research. Never ask the image model to imitate a living artist or reproduce a recognizable existing composition.

## Extract principles

Summarize the reference along these axes:

```text
story priority / attention hierarchy / negative-space function /
metaphor density / near-far behavior / figure-to-world scale /
material necessity / palette restraint / typography behavior /
pre-view question / post-view reward
```

Keep only observations supported by multiple works, a direct interview, or supplied reference images. Distinguish observation from inference.

## Separate principles from signatures

Reusable principles include:

- finding the film's dramatic core before choosing imagery;
- compressing the story into one symbol or visual paradox;
- making empty space carry distance, tension, silence, fate, or scale;
- letting the world or motif dominate instead of defaulting to a star portrait;
- creating one thumbnail read and one close-inspection discovery;
- translating culture through meaning, not decoration;
- using restrained color and typography to preserve hierarchy;
- designing for curiosity before viewing and recognition after viewing.

Do not copy signatures such as an exact motif, placement, silhouette, palette, brush treatment, title lockup, or known poster trick.

## Compile a neutral recipe

Translate the research into visible instructions without naming the source:

```text
one film-specific symbol;
large active negative space;
small human scale unless the story requires intimacy;
one visual paradox or containment relationship;
one culturally justified material process;
one dominant neutral family plus one emotional accent;
restrained title placement outside the metaphor;
no star montage or generic spectacle.
```

Adapt or discard every line according to the film. This is not a preset.

## Define the originality delta

Change at least four of these axes from every recognizable reference:

1. primary subject;
2. composition family;
3. metaphor operation;
4. spatial relationship;
5. material process;
6. palette logic;
7. typography behavior;
8. emotional temperature.

If the result is still recognizable because of one famous trick, reject it and choose a different metaphor operation.

## Huang Hai research translation

Use these as high-level observations, not as a named generation style:

- begin with the film's core rather than a fixed house style;
- use meaningful blank space and an airy, elusive atmosphere;
- often reduce the dominance of characters so the film world can speak;
- favor a concise symbolic image that can hold more than one interpretation;
- connect Eastern cultural thinking to the film only when the connection is necessary;
- avoid adding ink painting or calligraphy merely to appear Chinese;
- make the poster invite curiosity before viewing and deepen emotion after viewing;
- seek a contemporary visual language for culturally grounded ideas.

For generation, rewrite these observations as concrete pixels and omit the artist's name.

Research basis:

- Japan Foundation interview on blank space, “empty spirit,” story-first design, and avoiding unrelated ink painting: https://www.jff.jpf.go.jp/article/huanghai/
- StarWars.com interview on cultural interpretation, introspection, and building one image around a philosophical core: https://www.starwars.com/news/huang-hai-star-wars-poster
- Shanghai International Film Festival profile on avoiding self-repetition and expressing Eastern culture through contemporary visual language: https://www.siff.com/english/content?aid=import-cms-3194

## Prompt scrub

Before calling image generation, verify:

- no artist, studio, or campaign name remains;
- the prompt describes visible geometry, scale, material, light, and color;
- the film-specific motif remains legible at thumbnail size;
- one metaphor dominates;
- no decorative cultural shorthand has slipped in;
- the originality delta is explicit in the concept notes.
