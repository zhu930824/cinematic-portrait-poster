# Art-direction routing and refinement

Use this reference to turn narrative DNA into a coherent visual language before choosing a layout. It combines transferable lessons from alternative screen-print posters, editorial Chinese poster systems, canvas-first design practice, and production-oriented image-generation workflows. Do not imitate a named designer. Convert every influence into visible geometry, material, scale, color, and typography behavior.

## Design thesis

Write one compact design thesis before proposing concepts:

```text
movement name / worldview / spatial law / scale law /
material law / color law / typography law / emotional residue
```

Name an original visual movement in one or two neutral words, such as `Quiet Gravity` or `Luminous Tension`. Do not name a genre, artist, studio, surface, or historical poster campaign.

The thesis is not a mood-board paragraph. It must make testable decisions:

- **worldview**: what the film believes about people, fate, memory, power, or place;
- **spatial law**: where pressure, silence, distance, enclosure, or release occurs;
- **scale law**: what is monumental and what is vulnerable;
- **material law**: which story-grounded surface carries the idea;
- **color law**: one dominant family and one purposeful accent;
- **typography law**: how the title and factual copy inhabit the same world;
- **emotional residue**: what remains after the first glance.

Keep the thesis to 80–140 words. Every public concept may vary its composition, but it must either express this thesis or declare a deliberate alternate thesis.

## Direction router

Choose one primary lane. A secondary lane may contribute one supporting behavior, never a second visual system.

| Lane | Select when | Geometry and scale | Material and color | Title behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Graphic symbol | the film can be compressed into one iconic contradiction | one focal shape, hard silhouette, large quiet field, often extreme small/large contrast | 2–5 functional colors; use crisp paint, clean offset, projection, or film-evidenced print texture | title acts as counterweight, cut, frame, or inscription |
| Poetic material | touch, memory, weather, craft, land, or domestic life carries the emotion | asymmetry, partial crop, slow near/far reveal | one evidenced surface such as rain, glass, cloth, earth, skin, foliage, or light; derive temperature from the story | title inherits reflection, pressure, weave, residue, refraction, or edge |
| Editorial evidence | archives, institutions, investigation, history, or cultural evidence matter | grid, rail, specimen field, measured interruption | clean diagram, projection, record, map, object label, or evidenced document; paper is optional | title behaves as label, record, measured object, or single intervention |
| Spatial myth | fate, childhood, fantasy, journey, or historical force exceeds the character | tiny human scale, immense world, vertical or diagonal passage | atmospheric layers with one legible mass; avoid generic spectacle | title becomes threshold, horizon, monument, or distant echo |
| Intimate portrait | face, body, or performance is truly the dramatic core | one partial portrait, decisive crop, one secondary object at most | restrained photography or illustration, controlled local contrast | title follows gaze, garment edge, shadow, or bodily axis |
| Typographic architecture | the official title itself can carry the film's conflict | characters become passages, barriers, containers, or rhythm | exact glyph skeleton plus one story-grounded material action | title is the primary structure; credits form a calm secondary rail |

Do not choose a lane because it looks fashionable. State why the film requires it.

## Symbolic compression patterns

Use one pattern as the dominant far read:

- **Single-shape story**: one emblem contains the dramatic proposition.
- **Vulnerable versus immense**: a small subject confronts a monumental object, void, title, or environmental force.
- **Hidden second image**: a void, reflection, crop, or overlap reveals a second meaning.
- **World in evidence**: a location, relationship, or time layer exists inside one object or trace.
- **One decisive anomaly**: repetition establishes a system; one difference carries the conflict.
- **Material causality**: a story action visibly changes a material, and that change reaches the title.

Apply these limits unless the film requires otherwise:

- no more than three major narrative masses;
- one primary focal point;
- one dominant metaphor operation;
- one beauty engine that remains attractive before the metaphor is decoded;
- a far read describable in five words;
- one close-read reward;
- one optional interruptor.

For the Graphic symbol lane, begin with 2–5 colors and add another only when it has a named narrative job. Limited color is a construction discipline, not a retro filter.

| recent-output delta | | | |

Prefer these concept roles:

1. **Symbol**: the story compressed into one unforgettable far-read image.
2. **Evidence**: one real object, material, or trace carrying emotional specificity.
3. **Structure**: space or title architecture expressing the conflict.

These roles complement the A/B/C native-title treatments; they do not replace them. Avoid three variations of the same large-title composition.

## Prompt production contract

Before generation, collect and freeze:

- exact official title and punctuation;
- exact approved English title, tagline, date, and factual copy, if any;
- verified credits and their sources;
- reference-image roles;
- output aspect ratio and safe area;
- surface design, story evidence, palette roles, paper/distress declarations, and recent-output delta;
- title route and the exact text zones assigned to the image model;
- invariants, must-avoid items, and originality delta.

Quote all text that the image model must render verbatim. Specify placement, hierarchy, glyph construction, and interaction. For difficult short titles, state character count and order. Keep credits, legal copy, dates, awards, logos, and billing deterministic.

In an edit pass, repeat all invariants and request one material change at a time. Do not rewrite the entire prompt when only the title field, silhouette, or accent color is wrong.

## Subtractive refinement

After the first viable result, refine in this order:

1. **Meaning**: remove anything not sourced from the film or design thesis.
2. **Silhouette**: make the far read survive at 10% size.
3. **Hierarchy**: allow one boss, one support, and one quiet field.
4. **Geometry**: correct margins, alignments, tangent collisions, crop pressure, and axis relationships.
5. **Material**: make one surface convincing; remove generic texture overlays.
6. **Color**: assign each color a function and reduce unassigned accents.
7. **Typography**: make title and credits share a structural axis without equal emphasis.
8. **Craft**: inspect edges, glyphs, faces, hands, microtext, and accidental marks at full size.

Default to subtraction, alignment, and clarification before adding new graphics. A refinement pass must name the defect it fixes. Reject requests such as “make it more cinematic” unless rewritten as a visible correction.

Run two final reads:

- **three-second read**: film identity, dominant emotion, and title are clear;
- **thirty-second read**: material detail, concealed relationship, and verified credits reward inspection.
