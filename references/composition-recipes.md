# Composition recipes

## Composition families

| Family | Geometry | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| solitary-horizon | very small figure, large field, distant boundary | journey, grief, historical forces |
| central-icon | one stable frontal emblem | myth, biography, ritual, animation |
| fate-diagonal | small and large subjects connected diagonally | pursuit, adventure, unequal bonds |
| fracture-stage | action embedded in a crack, cut, or torn plane | repair, trauma, investigation |
| object-world | narrative contained inside one object silhouette | history, memory, profession |
| double-realm | upper/lower or real/reflected worlds | fantasy, psychology, secrets |
| negative-reveal | dominant mass plus hidden void image | absence, identity, mystery |
| vertical-scroll | eye travels through layered time or landscape | epic, road film, period story |
| title-monument | title or glyph forms the primary structure | bold teaser, history, martial arts |
| partial-colossus | a hand, eye, garment, or tool fills the frame | intimacy, threat, character study |
| collective-symbol | people form a meaningful shared shape | ensemble, social history, documentary |
| evidence-field | one object or ordered set behaves like evidence | crime, mystery, procedural |

## Material families

- ink wash and dry brush: use when writing, weather, movement, or moral ambiguity is story-relevant;
- woodcut, rubbing, paper cut, embroidery, lacquer, ceramic crackle: use only when grounded in period, place, craft, or subject;
- restrained photography plus flat graphic intervention: use for contemporary drama, romance, family, or realism;
- archive paper, contact sheet, stamped record, specimen: use for documentary, history, investigation;
- painterly surrealism, translucent layers, inverted landscape: use for fantasy, animation, science fiction, or psychological stories.

## Genre biases

- **Drama/family**: partial-colossus, object-world, negative-reveal; warm worn neutrals with one emotional color.
- **Romance**: shared contour, containment, temporal fold; avoid generic embracing couples and pink haze.
- **Mystery/crime**: evidence-field, fracture-stage, repetition/anomaly; avoid generic red strings, fingerprints, and silhouetted detectives.
- **Horror**: negative-reveal, substitution, partial-colossus; use withheld information rather than gore by default.
- **History/biography**: vertical-scroll, central-icon, object-world; ground every traditional material in evidence.
- **Wuxia**: fate-diagonal, title-monument, solitary-horizon; favor force, rhythm, and spatial tension over decorative ink.
- **Fantasy/animation**: double-realm, scale reversal, material transmutation; allow richer color while preserving one focal hierarchy.
- **Science fiction**: scale reversal, containment, temporal fold; avoid automatic neon, holographic UI, and blue-orange spectacle.
- **Documentary**: archive or craft materials, collective-symbol, object-world; do not fictionalize evidence.

## Palette logic

Assign functions before colors:

```text
ground = time and environment
structure = visual skeleton
emotion = psychological temperature
fate accent = one decisive high-chroma event
```

Use one dominant family and one accent. Suggested accent share:

- literary/festival: 1–5%;
- mystery/horror: 3–8%;
- commercial drama: 5–12%;
- fantasy/animation: 8–20%.

These are heuristics, not quotas. Preserve thumbnail contrast.
