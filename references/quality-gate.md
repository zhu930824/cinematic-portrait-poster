# Quality gate

## Story

- Can the concept be explained in one sentence?
- Is the primary motif evidenced by the film?
- Does the poster express conflict or emotional promise rather than retell the plot?
- Is spoiler risk appropriate to the campaign stage?
- Would the concept still fit many unrelated films after changing only the title? If yes, revise.

## Image

- Is the art-direction lane required by the film rather than selected as a fashionable look?
- Can the far read be described in five words?
- Are there no more than three major narrative masses?
- Is there one primary visual mechanism?
- Does the silhouette or contrast survive at thumbnail size?
- Does the near read reward closer inspection?
- Does negative space carry tension, absence, distance, or scale?
- Is the quiet field a named story-world substance rather than default warm paper?
- Does the ground have direct story evidence?
- If paper, fading, stains, grain, halftone, or misregistration appears, is each necessary rather than a prestige shortcut?
- Does the design avoid stacking aged paper, bone white, charcoal, vermilion, screen print, paper tooth, and ink misregistration by default?
- Does the surface differ materially from the last two completed posters unless repetition is story-required?
- Are traditional materials culturally and narratively grounded?
- Are there generated text artifacts, extra limbs, false faces, or accidental logos?
- Does the structure avoid recognizable imitation of an existing poster?
- Would the concept still work without cinematic glow, debris, smoke, or an action pose?
- If an authored reference was supplied, were its name and recognizable signatures removed from the generation prompt?
- Does the originality delta change at least four axes from the reference?

## Art direction and refinement

- Does the design thesis make testable spatial, scale, material, color, and typography decisions?
- Do the three concepts occupy Symbol, Evidence, and Structure roles or show equivalent structural diversity?
- Do they differ across at least four axes in the concept diversity matrix?
- Does every visible accent, texture, and traditional material have a named story function?
- Did each regeneration or edit address one named defect while preserving invariants?
- Was subtraction, alignment, or clarification attempted before adding another graphic?
- At the three-second read, are film identity, dominant emotion, and title clear?
- At the thirty-second read, do material detail, concealed meaning, and verified credits reward inspection?

## Typography and facts

- Does the final poster visibly contain the exact film title?
- Is the title-production route recorded as Native expressive, Native controlled, or Deterministic exact?
- For a native title, were every character, order, language variant, punctuation mark, false radical, and duplicate checked at full size?
- If the native title failed, was it regenerated instead of hidden beneath an ordinary overlay?
- Does the title participate in the composition rather than sit in an arbitrary leftover corner?
- Does the title use one story-grounded action rather than several decorative effects?
- Is there one observable primary material or evidence field?
- Does the text reserve have a named purpose rather than behaving as unexplained emptiness?
- Does the poster remain visually compelling when the title is mentally hidden?
- Would the title mechanism fail if moved unchanged to an unrelated film?
- Has the exact official title been preserved unless an alternate campaign title was explicitly approved?
- In a series, does the title mechanism avoid repeating either of the two previous structures?
- Does the final poster contain verified director and principal-cast credits, plus the screenwriter when reliably available?
- Are `creator_credit`, `verified_cast`, and `verified_credits` all present and visibly distinct?
- Does the complete credit system occupy a deliberate 12–22% reserve rather than one tiny afterthought?
- Is the director an authored signature, entry point, or counterweight rather than a name buried in the bottom block?
- Do principal-cast names form a readable rail or rhythm rather than legal microcopy?
- Does the compact block act as a factual base, threshold, or evidence label rather than carrying the whole hierarchy?
- Do the credit groups share an axis, edge, rail, horizon, or alignment with the title and primary motif?
- Does `credit_design` state a concrete and different function for creator, cast, and factual-base groups?
- Would separately removing the creator signature or cast rail weaken balance, direction, rhythm, or authorship?
- Are creator, cast, and compact credit sizes at or above 1.4%, 1.5%, and 1.25% of poster width?
- Are the creator signature and cast rail both larger than the compact factual base?
- Are principal names readable at phone width without zooming?
- If separate `creator_credit` or `verified_cast` groups are used, are they sourced and consistent with the mandatory `verified_credits` block?
- Is the credit source recorded, and are names, roles, language variant, punctuation, and billing order accurate?
- Are title, names, date, punctuation, and language variant exact?
- Are all awards, laurels, logos, ratings, and credits supplied or verified?
- Is every placeholder disclosed?
- Does text remain inside the safe area and avoid important faces or motifs?
- Was Chinese glyph coverage checked after font fallback?
- Is either the text-free key art or accepted title-bearing poster plate retained alongside the final exact-copy poster?
- Is `surface_design` complete and consistent with the visible ground, process, palette, quiet field, paper use, and distress use?

## Delivery

- Confirm aspect ratio and output dimensions.
- State the selected recipe and design interpretation.
- Include the exact prompt used for generation.
- Disclose font substitutions, omitted facts, and unresolved production constraints.
- For a series, verify shared motif, palette, and type system plus meaningful variation.
